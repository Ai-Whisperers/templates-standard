These **modern competitive practices** are what economists and strategists call **“non-price competition levers”** — they manipulate the *structure of advantage* instead of the *price of the product*.
Here’s how to define them conceptually and operationally:

---

### 1. **Data Network Effects**

**Definition:**
A feedback loop where every additional user or transaction improves the product’s quality or intelligence (via data aggregation), reinforcing dominance.
**Nature:** Economic-technical hybrid.
**Core function:** Converts usage into defensibility.
**Strategic class:** *Dynamic capabilities / learning economies.*

---

### 2. **Algorithmic Opacity**

**Definition:**
Deliberate concealment of internal algorithms, ranking logic, or model weights to retain asymmetric power over users and partners.
**Nature:** Information asymmetry as market power.
**Strategic class:** *Information control / opacity leverage.*

---

### 3. **Ecosystem Envelopment**

**Definition:**
Absorbing adjacent services or layers of the value chain, turning complements into dependencies.
**Nature:** Strategic bundling and vertical integration.
**Strategic class:** *Platform envelopment / boundary expansion.*

---

### 4. **Continuous Integration Drift**

**Definition:**
Silent API or SDK version drift that slowly breaks interoperability, keeping competitors in a reactive state.
**Nature:** Technical entropy as a weapon.
**Strategic class:** *Temporal lock-in / technical churn management.*

---

### 5. **Information Asymmetry Control**

**Definition:**
Manipulating access to metrics, data, or definitions to influence perception or regulation.
**Nature:** Narrative economics + selective transparency.
**Strategic class:** *Perception management / regulatory gaming.*

---

### 6. **Standard Capture**

**Definition:**
Dominating or co-opting standardization bodies to shape “open” protocols that subtly favor proprietary implementations.
**Nature:** Institutional lock-in.
**Strategic class:** *Meta-competition / rule-shaping.*

---

### 7. **Strategic Interoperability**

**Definition:**
Allowing controlled interoperability — enough to attract users, not enough to allow substitution.
**Nature:** Partial openness as bait.
**Strategic class:** *Asymmetric openness / containment design.*

---

### 8. **Ecosystem Gravity (Social Lock-in)**

**Definition:**
Dependency that arises from user identity, social graph, or community participation rather than functionality.
**Nature:** Cognitive + social network effects.
**Strategic class:** *Socio-technical inertia.*

---

### 9. **Loss-Leader Infrastructure**

**Definition:**
Offering critical infrastructure below cost to monopolize adoption, later monetizing the ecosystem.
**Nature:** Predatory pricing with deferred monetization.
**Strategic class:** *Cross-subsidized capture.*

---

### 10. **Regulatory Arbitrage**

**Definition:**
Exploiting differences or lags in regulatory frameworks between jurisdictions to gain time or market share.
**Nature:** Legal-geopolitical asymmetry.
**Strategic class:** *Temporal advantage / compliance arbitrage.*

---

### 🧩 Synthesis

All these practices fall under the modern meta-category of **“ecosystemic strategy”** — they build *gravitational pull* rather than mere market share.
They are **self-reinforcing**, **hard to detect early**, and **almost impossible to regulate reactively** because they operate below the price or product level — they shape **incentive geometry**.
